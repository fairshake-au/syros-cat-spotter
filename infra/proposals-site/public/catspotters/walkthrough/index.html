Cat Spotters, four weeks on

You tested Cat Spotters on 29 September. Since then we've worked through everything you told us, made the app calmer and clearer for spotters, and built the team dashboard Syros Cats will use day to day. This page walks through the things people actually do, step by step, with what's new and what's coming next for each.

The screens below are today's latest version, filled with sample data and real photos of Syros cats that your testers took. Tap any screen to see it full size.

Part one · the phone app

What people do in the app

Spotters are locals and visitors who photograph the cats they meet. Every photo helps Syros Cats see which cats are sterilised and where to trap next. Seven things people do, in the order they meet them.

App · 1

Getting started

Anyone opening the app for the first time, in English, Greek or French.

New since you tested

  • A quicker start: three short screens before the camera, down from five, in the phone's own language.
  • Your words up front: "Look, don't touch" and "Please don't feed them", as Syros Cats wrote them.
  • Works on poor wifi: "Start anyway, we'll save it later" on café or ferry connections.
More changes (1)
  • Location is only asked for at the first photo, when there's a reason.
App · 2

Spotting a cat

The heart of the app: photo, place, "have we met?", then a name or a match.

New since you tested

  • Easier to get a good photo: one Take a photo button, a short photo guide, and optional face and body photos.
  • Fewer duplicate cats: "Find the cat yourself" when the suggested cats are wrong.
  • Nothing gets lost: spots wait for signal, and an unfinished spot stays on the phone.
More changes (3)
  • An honest message when the photo check fails, instead of "you're the first".
  • Naming limits: you must have met the cat, up to 3 names a day, no links or phone numbers.
  • A cat fact while the photo is checked, in English, Greek or French. Facts you haven't approved stay hidden.

Part two · the team dashboard

Dashboard 101

The dashboard is Syros Cats' side of the app: every cat, every report from the public, colonies, vet visits and the numbers. It's a website, so it works on a laptop or a phone.

Where: team.catspotters.com  ·  Who: people Syros Cats invites. Each person has a role. Team members see everything; a cat guardian sees only their own colonies and cats.  ·  Help: every page now opens with a short line about what it's for, a "How this page works" fold, and a Words we use page for the terms.

Dashboard · 1

Home, and finding your way

The first page after signing in.

What it is

The first page after signing in. It answers three questions: what needs a person today, which colony to trap next, and how the island is doing.

How it helps

Nobody has to go looking. Urgent reports and kittens come first. Where to trap next puts the colonies with the most cats still to sterilise at the top, with what that would cost at €60 a cat. How we're doing gives the island's sterilisation figure at a glance. Every page explains itself in a short "How this page works" fold, and there's a Words we use page. It works on a phone and in dark mode.

In the proposal

Section 1 called the app a working tool for your team: which cats are sterilised, which colonies need attention, which cat was just reported injured. Home puts all three on one page.

Dashboard · 2

Finding a cat

Looking up a cat by name, look, place or status, or from a photo someone sent you.

What it is

Every cat as a photo grid. Narrow it by colour, pattern, area, colony, whether it's sterilised, and where it lives. Or use Find from a photo: add any photo and see which known cats it looks like. A report in the Queue links straight to it.

How it helps

It answers "do we already know this cat?" in seconds, whether a photo arrives by message or in a report. Checking first stops the same cat being counted twice.

In the proposal

Section 5 asked for a cat's look to be chosen from lists, "rather than typed", so the record can be searched in any language. Section 6 called duplicates "the single biggest threat to your numbers".

Dashboard · 3

A cat's record

Everything known about one cat, and the buttons to change it.

What it is

Everything known about one cat: photos, where it's seen, whether it's sterilised, how it lives, its colony, and its full history. Buttons to log a vet visit, update the cat, add a team note, or record that it has died.

How it helps

One place to check before trapping or answering a question about a cat. Nothing is overwritten: every change joins the history with who made it and when, so you can see how an answer was reached and undo a mistake.

In the proposal

Sections 5 and 6: each cat has its own page and history. A vet or your team makes a fact Confirmed, a clipped ear seen by several people makes it Probably, and "nothing is ever deleted". "This cat has died" came from your testing call.

Dashboard · 4

The Queue: reports from the public

Where "A cat needs help" reports and photo problems land.

What it is

Where the public's reports land: hurt or sick cats, kittens, and problems with a photo or a cat's name. Each report moves from new, to looking at it, to done.

How it helps

Every report reaches a person, and whoever sent it hears back. Before any email goes, the dashboard shows exactly what they'll receive. A photo someone has reported stays hidden until the team has looked.

In the proposal

Section 8: a report "must reach a person", and the person who reported it "is told what happened". Section 7: a queue for reported photos and names, "with the power to hide a photograph or rename a cat". The proposal left open who should be told about an urgent report. That's one of the questions at the end of this page.

Dashboard · 5

Colonies and trapping plans

Planning where to trap, and keeping each colony's cats and feeding sites straight.

What it is

Colonies are groups of cats that live in the same place. Some also have feeding sites or a cat guardian. Each colony shows how close it is to every cat sterilised, and has a trapping plan.

How it helps

Trapping rounds are planned from the record, not from memory. The plan lists the cats still to do, where each is usually seen and when it was last seen, and what the round would cost. It prints on one page to take out, with a tick box per cat.

In the proposal

Section 9: feeding sites double as "a map of where the cats you need to trap can reliably be found". Section 10: "choose a colony" and the app has "most of a target list, assembled without anybody compiling it".

Dashboard · 6

Vet visits and clinic days

Recording sterilisations and what they cost.

What it is

Record a vet visit on one cat, or a whole clinic day in one go, matching each cat's photo to a known cat or adding it as new. A standard price per sterilisation (€60 today) fills in the cost, and can be changed for a visit that cost something else.

How it helps

Each cat's record updates itself, so the sterilisation figures stay right without a separate spreadsheet, and the cost per cat adds up for your accounts and funders.

In the proposal

Section 10, "what a cat has cost": vet visits are recorded against the cat they were for. Section 11 also suggested letting the island's vets record sterilisations themselves. That idea isn't built yet.

Dashboard · 7

The island map and areas

Seeing where cats and feeding sites are across Syros.

What it is

Every cat and feeding site on one map of Syros, using the same filters as the photo grid. Place names show in your own language: English with the Greek beneath, or Greek. Named areas, such as Ermoupoli or Kini, which the team can edit.

How it helps

See where cats that still need sterilising are clustered, and which parts of the island nobody has photographed yet. Feeding sites and area centres are placed by dragging a pin on the map.

In the proposal

Section 11: show "which parts of Syros nobody has photographed". Section 9: the feeding-site map. Section 15: the public only ever sees a cat as a rough area, never an exact spot.

Dashboard · 8

Reports

The numbers, for planning, funders and the accounts.

What it is

Five reports: sterilisation progress, colony health, coverage and spotting, welfare patterns, and vet visits and costs. Pick the dates, and download any of them as a spreadsheet.

How it helps

Figures you can put in front of a funder or the municipality, each saying what it counts. For example, "confirmed sterilised" counts only cats a vet or your team confirmed, never a guess.

In the proposal

Section 11, "proving what you have achieved": figures that carry their own confidence, and answers to questions like "how many cats did we sterilise between January and December", with the method stated.

What a cat guardian sees

Optional, and your call. It only switches on for the cat guardians you choose to invite.

What it is

Cat guardians sign in to the same dashboard and see only their own colonies: their cats, their feeding sites (always private) and the counts.

A guardian can say a cat is sterilised, has a home, is in care or has died, in one tap, and undo it within 30 days. Their word counts for more than a passing visitor's; a vet's counts most. A new guardian can start their first colony.

A bigger question

Where we need your view before we build

This depends on how Syros Cats works, so we'd rather ask than guess.

Coat colours and patterns

The app describes every cat's coat by its colours and one pattern: plain, stripes or patches. On the team's pages, a patchy black-and-ginger cat also gets a familiar name: Tortoiseshell, or Calico if it has white.

What we plan to change

People, and the app's photo reader, keep mixing up some colours. So we plan to:

  • turn Black into Black or dark grey, including charcoal, mid grey and the dark stripes on a tabby
  • turn Ginger into Ginger or cream, including pale, washed-out ginger
  • keep light grey separate

That means more cats count as "black and ginger", so more would be called tortoiseshell or calico. Most rightly, but not all.

Three ways we could do it

A. Name tortoiseshells and calicos only

Includes pale grey-and-cream ones. A brown tabby with ginger patches (a "torbie") is described in plain words: "black, brown and ginger patches".

B. Name torbies too

Closest to how experts talk, but "torbie" is a word many people don't know, and some sandy tabbies would be named wrongly.

C. No names at all

Always plain colours. Never wrong, but the team loses the familiar names.

Our questions for you

  1. Do your volunteers and vets use the words tortoiseshell, calico or torbie for the island's cats? Which ones?
  2. Would you rather see torbies named (option B), even if a few sandy tabbies get it wrong?
  3. Do you prefer "Calico" or "Tortoiseshell and white"?
