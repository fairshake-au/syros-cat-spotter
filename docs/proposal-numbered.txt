   1  Syros has a large population of street cats and a small number of people working to care for them. The gap between those two facts is information. Nobody currently knows which cats exist, where they live, which of them have been sterilised, which need help, or where the next effort would do the most good.

   2  There are thousands of people on the island every summer who like cats, carry a camera and walk everywhere. This app turns that attention into a record.

   3  The people it is built for

   4  Visitors experience it as a game. They photograph a cat, find out whether it is one the island already knows, name it if it is new, collect the ones they have met, and see how they rank against other people.

   5  Residents experience it as recognition. The cat somebody has fed for six years gets a name, a page and a history, and the person who feeds it is named on that page.

   6  Families staying for a season , such as those who come to the island with Boundless Life, are a distinct group and matter more than their numbers suggest. They are here for one to three months rather than four days, they range far beyond the harbour, and their children have structured time. They are the group most likely to reach the parts of Syros that short-stay visitors never walk through.

   7  Syros Cats and its volunteers experience none of the above. For your team this is a working record and a tool. It tells you which cats are sterilised, which colonies need attention, which animal somebody has just reported as injured, and what you can put in front of a funder at the end of the year. Nobody on your team is using this app for entertainment, and it has not been designed as though they were.

   8  Two ideas that run through the whole design

   9  The first is citizen science . That phrase describes ordinary people, with no scientific training, collecting real observations that researchers or organisations then use. Birdwatchers counting garden birds on a set weekend each year is the best known example, and the results are used in genuine population studies.   10  The second is that raising engagement and money for Syros Cats is an explicit objective of this app, not a side effect . A tourist who has met and named a cat is far more likely to give than one who has read a poster, and the app is deliberately built to convert affection for a specific animal into support for the organisation that cares for it. Sections 15 and 17 are about that directly.

  11  The needs of these groups fit together rather than competing. The public side has to stay effortless, because a visitor who finds it tedious simply stops, and every record your team relies on comes from somebody enjoying themselves. The more enjoyable the app is, the more data you get, the better your reporting becomes, and the more compelling the case for donating. Each part feeds the next.

  61  Cats that quietly disappear

  62  Most street cats are never confirmed dead. They simply stop being seen. If the app does nothing about that, the catalogue grows for ever, the island's cat count climbs past anything real, and every percentage calculated against it slowly becomes meaningless.

  63  So the app assumes a lifespan. After three years with no sighting at all, a cat moves automatically to “presumed deceased”. It leaves the active population count and stops appearing as a cat to go and find, while staying fully in the record and the history.   66  Everybody using the app is collecting observations about cats, including their health, and we want as many of those as possible. The design question is not who is allowed to contribute.   67  A tourist, a resident, a volunteer and a vet can all say something about the same cat, and they are not making the same kind of claim. “I think I saw a clipped ear” and “I sterilised this cat on 14 May” are different in kind.   68  So the app keeps every observation rather than replacing one with another. Each is dated, attributed, and usually carries the photograph that prompted it. The cat's status is then worked out from all of them together, and the app always shows how confident it is.

 179  You asked a good question about this, and it deserves a direct answer. Fair Shake would be providing and operating this app for you as a service, which affects how responsibility is divided.

 180  Data protection law distinguishes between the organisation that decides why personal data is collected, called the controller, and the organisation that handles it on the first one's instructions , called the processor. The controller carries most of the legal obligations, and the processor carries a narrower but real set of its own.

 186  We would therefore propose showing only an approximate location to the anonymous public for any cat you flag as at risk.
