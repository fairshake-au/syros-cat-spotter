   1  Syros has a large population of street cats and a small number of people working to care for them. The gap between those two facts is information: which cats exist, where they live, which have been sterilised, which need help, and where the next effort would do the most good.

   2  There are thousands of people on the island every summer who like cats, carry a camera, and walk everywhere. The app turns that attention into a record.

   3  For a visitor , it's a game: photograph a cat, find out whether it's one the island already knows, name it if it's new, collect the ones you've met, and see how you rank.

   4  For a resident , it's recognition: the cat you've fed for six years gets a name, a page and a history, and you're on it.

   5  For Syros Cats and its volunteers , it is none of those things. It is a working record and a tool: which cats are sterilised, which colonies need attention, which animal somebody has just reported as injured, and what you can put in front of a funder at the end of the year. Nobody on your team is using this app for entertainment, and it is not designed as though they were.

   6  The app therefore serves two audiences with opposite needs. For the public it has to be effortless, or they stop and there is no data at all. For your team it has to be dependable, precise and quick to work with. Where the two pull against each other, we keep the public side effortless, because everything your team gets out of the app depends on the public putting things into it.

   7  Worth settling early, because an app store name is painful to change once people have started recommending it to each other, and because the words we choose have to work in Greek, English and French.

  38  Cats that quietly disappear

  39  Most street cats are never confirmed dead. They simply stop being seen. If the app does nothing about that, the catalogue grows for ever, the island's cat count climbs past anything real, and every percentage calculated against it slowly becomes meaningless.

  40  So the app assumes a lifespan. After a set period with no sighting at all, a cat moves automatically to presumed deceased : it leaves the active population count, stops appearing as a cat to go and find, and stays fully in the record and the history. If anyone photographs it the next day, it returns to its previous status immediately and nothing is lost.

  42  This is the part we'd most like you to understand, because it's what makes your numbers defensible.

  43  Everyone using the app is collecting observations about cats, including their health, and we want as many of those as possible. The design question is not who is allowed to contribute. It is how the app turns a large number of contributions of very different reliability into one answer that is worth publishing.

  44  A tourist, a resident, a volunteer and a vet can all say something about the same cat, and they are not making the same kind of claim. “I think I saw a clipped ear” and “I sterilised this cat on 14 May” differ in kind. If the app simply let each new contribution overwrite the last, a vet's record could be replaced by a guess, and your figures would be worth nothing to a funder.

  45  So the app keeps every observation rather than replacing one with another. Each is dated, attributed, and usually carries the photograph that prompted it. The cat's status is then worked out from all of them together, and the app always shows how confident it is:

  46  Level Means Example Confirmed Someone authoritative said so A vet recorded the operation Likely Several people independently agree Three spotters have seen a clipped ear Reported One person, unconfirmed A spotter thinks she may be pregnant Unknown Nobody has said Most cats, at the start

  47  The practical effect is that the data improves on its own as the app is used. Every additional observation moves a cat closer to a settled answer, without anybody having to review anything. A cat nobody has looked at closely sits at “unknown”, and a cat several people have independently reported the same thing about becomes “likely” by itself.

  48  Authority is specific rather than general. A vet's word settles whether a cat has been sterilised and carries no special weight on where it sleeps. A guardian's word about their own cat's condition outranks a passing spotter's, and never outranks a vet's on surgery.

  49  Nothing is ever deleted. A correction is added on top and the original stays visible, so the record shows how an answer was reached rather than hiding the disagreement.

 100  Real science, done by children

 101  This is the idea we'd most like to build the educational side around, because it is the one that makes the app worth a school's or a family's time rather than merely enjoyable.

 102  A child using this app is not pretending to be a scientist. They are doing the actual work: observing an animal carefully, recording what they see, distinguishing one individual from another, noticing that their evidence can be wrong, and contributing to a dataset that a real organisation uses to make real decisions. That is what field biology is, and almost no child ever gets to do it.

 146  So we'd propose showing an approximate location publicly for any cat you flag as at risk, while the exact location stays visible to Syros Cats, your volunteers, the cat's guardian, and any signed-in user you choose to trust . The blurring applies only to the anonymous public view, so it never gets in the way of the people who need to find the animal.

 163  Someone who has spent a week meeting the cats of Syros forms a genuine attachment to specific animals. At the moment they leave, they want something, and there is nothing to give them.

 164  The idea is to let people order a physical keepsake showing a cat they photographed, printed and posted to their home address by a print-on-demand partner: a magnet, a mug, a print, a tote bag, a set of postcards.  165  How it would make money. Print-on-demand partners charge a base cost per item and let the seller set the retail price, so the difference is yours.
