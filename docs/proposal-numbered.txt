   1  Syros has a large population of street cats and a small number of people caring for them. The gap between those two facts is information. Nobody knows how many cats there are, which have been sterilised, which are in trouble, or where the next effort would do the most good.

   2  The island is full of tourists, including families with children who are curious about the cats on the island. Many of them walk around the island with phones in their pockets, and use them to take photos of the cats out of curiosity and affection for them.

   3  This app turns that scattered affection into the record you are missing, and then turns that curiosity and affection itself into support for your work.

   5  The people it serves

   6  Visitors experience it as a game. They photograph cats, collect the ones they meet, name the new ones and see how they rank, and they learn about the cats in the process.

   7  Families staying for a season , such as those who come with Boundless Life, are a distinct group. They stay for months rather than four days, and their children have structured time. They want to contribute to the community and to learn while they're here.

   8  Residents experience it as activity that helps improve amenity and wellbeing across the island. The cat somebody has fed for six years gets a name, a page and a history, and they see that your team is using the data to address the challenges that wild cat populations create.

   9  Syros Cats and its volunteers experience none of the above. For your team this is a working tool: which cats are sterilised, which colonies need attention, which animal was just reported injured, and what data you can put in front of a funder.

  10  These needs fit together. The public side has to stay effortless, because every record your team relies on comes from somebody enjoying themselves. The more enjoyable the app is, the more data you get, the better your reporting becomes, and the stronger the data you have to support impact reporting and funding.

  11  Two ideas that run through everything

  12  The first is citizen science , which means ordinary people, with no training, collecting real observations that an organisation then uses. Birdwatchers counting garden birds on one weekend a year is the best-known example, and their counts feed genuine population studies. A visitor photographing a cat in an alley is doing the same thing. That is what makes this app worth a school's time, not just a tourist's.

  13  The second is that raising engagement and donation income for Syros Cats is an explicit objective, not a side effect . A tourist who has met and named a cat gives more readily than one who has read a poster, and the app is built to convert affection for a specific animal into support for the organisation caring for all of them.

  49  When kittens arrive

  50  The app treats a litter as its own kind of report, and anybody can make one.

  51  Reporting a litter. The moment the app sees a kitten in any photograph, even a single one, it opens the "litter flow" and prompts for the two facts that matter most. First, how many kittens can you see altogether, since where there is one there are usually more. Second, can you find the mother: she is usually close by, and the app asks the person to look for her and photograph her, then matches her against known cats

  52  What the report creates. A litter record with a place, a date, a count, an age estimate and, where known, a link to the mother. The litter gets a page of its own, like a cat does. What it deliberately does not get is a page per kitten, because young kittens cannot be told apart from photographs, they change appearance within weeks, and sadly not all of them survive.

  53  Nothing is thrown away. The distinction is not between what the app keeps and what it discards, because it keeps everything. The distinction is what an observation attaches to. While the kittens might be too young to tell apart, every photograph and every check-in attaches to the litter. Once a kitten is old enough to be recognised as an individual, its sightings attach to it instead. The early history is never lost:

 149  Who is responsible under GDPR

 150  Because Fair Shake would provide and operate the app for you as a managed service, responsibility divides. Data protection law distinguishes the organisation that decides why data is collected, called the controller, from the organisation that handles it on the first one's instructions, called the processor. The controller carries most of the obligations, and the processor carries a narrower set of its own.

 151  Most likely position Syros Cats Controller for the data collected for your work: guardians, feeders and volunteers. You decide why those records exist. Fair Shake Processor, operating the app on your instructions, and controller only for the little data that exists to run the service itself.
