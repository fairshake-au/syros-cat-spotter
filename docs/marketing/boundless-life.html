A field study on the doorstep, for the length of a stay

Real science for children who are already curious about the cats they walk past every day — and a contribution to the island that would not happen without them.

For Boundless Life — education and community teams Syros, Greece September 2026

What the app is

Syros has a large population of street cats, a small number of people caring for them, and no record of how many there are or which have been sterilised. Cat Spotters closes that gap by turning the photographs people already take into a record the island can use.

You photograph a cat and the app answers one question: have we met this one before? It shows the closest matches with a plain reason — “the white bib and the notch in the left ear match” — and you confirm one or name a cat nobody has met. Spotters earn points, badges and streaks, the same machinery Duolingo uses. It opens in a browser at catspotters.com and installs from the app stores, runs in English, Greek and French, and needs no account.

Why a cohort is a distinctive contributor

Everybody maps the island, and the app depends on that: residents watch the same streets across years, tourists cover the harbour thoroughly. Families staying for a season reach what those two leave — children with structured time and no fixed itinerary range across Ano Syros, Vari, Galissas and Kini in a way nobody does in four days. Because the record persists, a cohort in April can also be told what happened to the cats a cohort in October first found.

What a cohort would do

Join with a code, take a territory

A group is created with a joining code, and anybody who enters it has their spotting counted toward the group. The cohort gets a neighbourhood and a target, and a coverage map shows where nobody has looked yet.

Watch the score, keep the result

Cats found, how many previously unknown, how much of the area covered. At the end of the stay that becomes a summary the group keeps, with a note from Syros Cats saying what the work led to.

Report what needs help

A cat in trouble takes seconds to report, and wherever it can the app closes the loop, so whoever made the report hears back. When a kitten appears, the children count the litter and look for the mother.

Learn while doing it

Teaching arrives as moments, not lessons: while a photograph is identified, the screen shows one short fact, where possible about the cat just photographed.

The learning underneath

Children using this app are not pretending to do science. They are observing an animal carefully, telling one individual from another, discovering their own evidence can be wrong, and contributing to a dataset that informs real decisions. That is field biology, and very few children get to do it — and it teaches that “I’m not sure” is a valuable answer rather than a failure.

Reading a catColours, patterns and body language: the flat ear, the slow blink, the upright tail as a greeting.
Life in a colonyWhat a colony is, how territory works, who eats first, where cats sleep and why.
Why sterilisation worksThe arithmetic of one unsterilised female, why removing cats fails, and why an ear tip is good news rather than an injury.
Being around a street catHow to approach one, when not to, and why not to share your lunch — the hardest one to explain to a child, and the one that matters most.

What a collaboration would look like

Boundless Life providesAn education contact, a group set up at each arrival, a mention in the welcome, and honest feedback on what works with different ages.
Syros Cats providesThe expertise behind everything the app teaches, the welfare response when a cohort reports a cat, and the note at the end of each stay.
Fair Shake providesThe app itself, in three languages, in a browser and on iOS and Android, plus the group features. Fair Shake designed it with Syros Cats on a pro bono basis.
Cost, and children’s dataNothing to pay, and nothing asks a family to spend money. A spotter is anonymous by default — no name, email address or account — and a child can take part fully that way.

What we would like to talk about

  • Does this fit a cohort’s week as a running project, a field day, or something families do on their own time? We have designed for all three.
  • A trial with one cohort, with a neighbourhood assigned and a summary at the end, before anybody commits to more.
  • Whether this travels. The app is not specific to Syros — it works at any site with street cats and a local organisation caring for them.
