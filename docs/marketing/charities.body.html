An app that turns a tourist’s photograph into an island-wide cat census — and turns the affection behind that photograph into greater engagement and impact. Designed by Fair Shake with Syros Cats, and ready to be handed to other organisations doing like work.

For animal-welfare organisations and the purpose sector September 2026

The gap we are closing

Every place facing cat overpopulation has two challenges: a large cat population, and a small number of people caring for them. The gap between them is information — nobody knows how many cats there are, which are sterilised, or where the next effort would do most good. Meanwhile thousands of visitors photograph those cats every summer out of pure affection, and none of it reaches anybody who could use it. Cat Spotters is the bridge: a gamified engagement app for the public and a working record for Syros Cats, where the same tap does both jobs.

How it works

A visitor photographs a tabby in Ano Syros, learns the island has never met her, and names her Agatha. Others confirm her over the summer, and one notices her ear is not clipped. By September, Syros Cats can see where the cats are, where hardly any are sterilised, and where a morning of volunteer time will do most good — and the round above Ano Syros brings up an unsterilised female: it is Agatha. In October the visitor, long since home, is told Agatha was sterilised last week, with a button underneath that pays for the next cat.

What the public side does

Cat Spotters opens in a browser at catspotters.com and installs from the App Store and Google Play. It runs in English, Greek and French, and nobody needs an account to take part.

Spot a cat

Photograph a cat and the app answers one question: have we met this one before? It shows the closest matches with a plain reason — “the white bib and the notch in the left ear match” — and the person confirms one or names a new cat. Every cat has a page: where it lives, its health, and everybody who has spotted it.

Points, badges, streaks

The same machinery Duolingo and other gamified apps use: what turns one pleasant moment into a habit, and the habit is what builds the record. Groups — a school, a family, a visiting cohort — join with a code and get their own figures.

Cats in trouble, and litters

Both take seconds to report, and when a kitten appears the app asks how many can be seen and prompts the spotter to find the mother. Wherever it can, the app closes the loop: the report reaches a person, and whoever made it hears back.

Teaching, and where to look

While a photograph is identified, the screen shows a short fact instead of a spinner — what a clipped ear means, why sterilisation is the humane answer, why sharing your lunch harms a colony in winter. A coverage map shows where nobody has looked, so a walk becomes a contribution.

The donation ladder

The two moments where feeling runs highest: on the spot, and later, when somebody hears their cat was helped. Each rung says what it buys, from €1 to feed a cat for a day to €50–60 to sterilise one.

What only Syros Cats sees

Behind the public side sits a working tool that only Syros Cats and approved users can see.

Invited rolesResidents sign themselves up. Cat guardian, volunteer, vet and management come by invitation only, so nobody can declare themselves a vet.
Defensible figuresEvery observation is kept, dated and attributed, and each cat’s status carries its confidence: confirmed by Syros Cats or the vet, likely where several people agree, reported by one, or unknown.
Trapping roundsOpen a colony and the target list is most of the way written: which cats are unsterilised, where each is found, which feeding site they gather at. Feeders’ details are recorded with consent.
Duplicate controlDuplicates impact the integrity of the data, which the app is designed to avoid: it surfaces likely duplicates and merges only on human confirmation.
Every sterilisation, not only yoursIf the island’s vets opt in, the reporting changes from “cats we have sterilised” to “cats on this island that are sterilised”.
Reporting and costCoverage colony by colony, exported with the method stated. Vet visits are recorded against the cat they were for, by anyone who makes this happen, so cost per cat adds up on its own.

Why it works

This is real citizen science — ordinary people with no training collecting observations that inform real decisions, the same idea as the garden bird counts that feed genuine population studies. It also teaches that evidence has different strengths, and that “I’m not sure” is a valuable answer.

Alongside that, the app shows why sterilisation is the best and most humane solution, then closes the loop by giving users the option to donate. A donation of €50–60 is a cat that will not have four more litters, and each of the kittens in those litters would need the same again. Users connect with a specific animal and can take tangible action to support it, which we think will improve engagement outcomes and donations.

Designed to be handed on

Nothing in the design is specific to Syros except the map. The same app runs anywhere there are street cats, an organisation that needs numbers it can defend, and visitors who like taking photographs. Fair Shake operates it as a managed service, and designed the app with Syros Cats on a pro bono basis.
