Five questions for the Syros testers

Every question is a score out of five and a box to say why. Five numbers you can track from one round of testing to the next, and five reasons that tell you what to do about them. Two minutes to answer, anonymous.

  • 5 scores + 5 reasons
  • ~2 minutes
  • English + Greek
  • Anonymous
  • Sent via WhatsApp

The survey

Same shape five times over: rate it 1–5, then tell us why. The orange notes are for you, not for them.

Build sheet

Ten fields, five pairs, in this order.

2bParagraph textOptional
3Multiple choice, 5 rows + "did not get this far"Required
3bParagraph textOptional
4Multiple choice, 5 rows + "did not get this far"Required
4bParagraph textOptional
5Multiple choice, 5 rowsRequired
5bShort answerRequired — the only compulsory text box
  1. Multiple choice, not a linear scale. A Google Forms linear scale only labels the two ends and will not take the "did not get this far" option. Five labelled rows cost nothing extra to tap and mean a 3 means the same thing to every tester.
  2. Two forms, English and Greek, both links in one message. A language question at the top costs a question you do not have spare, and mixed-language answer rows get mis-tapped. Merge the responses afterwards.
  3. No email or name field. Email capture is a designed, purposeful moment inside the app — bolting it onto a feedback form is how you teach people the app collects things quietly.
  4. The Greek needs a native read before you send it. It is faithful, but a tester survey is the wrong place to discover a clumsy phrase, and the Greek and French review is already an open item.
  5. Open for a week, one reminder. A group like this answers on the first day or not at all; one nudge roughly doubles the count, a second annoys people you need.

Reading the answers

  1. Count the "did not get this far" answers first. They are your drop-off: motivated people who never reached matching, never got outdoors with it, or never understood it. A high count on question 2 means the problem is upstream in question 1's loop, whatever question 1 scored.
  2. Average questions 2 and 4 only over the people who actually got there. Counting a "did not get this far" as a zero, or leaving it out of the denominator by accident, will flatter or wreck the number either way.
  3. Question 1 below 4 outranks everything. The whole product is that loop repeated, so a weak score there makes the other four academic until it is fixed.
  4. Question 5's text clusters or it does not. Three or more testers naming the same thing is a launch blocker; a scatter of singletons means the basics hold and those go to the backlog.
  5. Hunt for the split scores. Someone who scored question 1 low and question 5 high pushed through real difficulty because they want this to exist — go and talk to them directly. They are worth more than the averages.
